NetSuite change

A pricing rule, operational screen, or batch process needs to change without losing sight of the business process around it.

  1. Understand the rule

    Trace the records, roles, and exceptions. Agree on the expected result and the existing behavior that the change must preserve.

  2. Choose the boundary

    Choose configuration, SuiteScript, a Suitelet, or a batch job based on the workflow, governance limits, and support needs.

  3. Check real cases

    Test representative records and edge cases. Review the diff, permissions, deployment objects, and effects on adjacent processes.

  4. Release with a plan

    Validate the target, define rollback, and check behavior after deployment. Leave operating notes that another developer can use.

The deliverable is a change the business can operate and the next developer can understand.

Integration recovery

Orders stop moving, inventory disagrees, or a retry creates another problem. Start by finding the exact boundary where the data diverges.

  1. Trace one record

    Follow a representative transaction from source to destination. Compare payloads, mappings, identifiers, and the last confirmed successful step.

  2. Make recovery safe

    Define which system owns each value, how duplicates are detected, and which failures can be retried without repeating a business action.

  3. Reconcile the result

    Check quantities, statuses, and record relationships across systems. Test the failure path as well as the successful transfer.

  4. Restore visibility

    Apply the correction to a bounded set, verify the destination, and document what operators should monitor and when to escalate.

A working connection includes a way to understand and recover from its failures.

AI delivery

I use AI agents for research, implementation, and review, with explicit task boundaries and human ownership of the result.

  1. Define the contract

    Bind the work to its project and current source. Write scope, acceptance criteria, constraints, and the evidence needed to call it complete.

  2. Divide the work

    Break the problem into dependent tasks. Assign focused roles and isolate parallel changes so builders can work without overwriting each other.

  3. Verify independently

    Treat agent reports as claims. Inspect code, run appropriate checks, and use a separate review to challenge assumptions and missed cases.

  4. Close with evidence

    Distinguish implemented, tested, and deployed work. Record unresolved limits and preserve useful findings for the next change.

AI contributes the work. I own the architecture, review, and delivery decisions.
